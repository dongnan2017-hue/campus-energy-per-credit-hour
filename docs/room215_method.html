How one classroom's electricity cost is calculated

A walk through Olin Hall room 215 at Albion College, from one campus-wide number down to a cost per credit hour for Fall 2026. Every input is marked as public data, an assumption, or arithmetic.

Modeled estimates, not meter readings. Albion publishes no building or room meter data, so the building and room figures divide up a single rounded campus total. Unofficial student project, not affiliated with or endorsed by Albion College.

Part 1. How much electricity does Olin Hall get?

Steps 1 to 7
1

Campus electricity

Public data
14,000,000 kWh × 100% modeled share
14,000,000 kWh

Albion's news article says the college uses "about 14 million kilowatt hours." It gives no time period, so I treat it as one year. The 100% share means all of it is spread over the 36 buildings in the model. Albion has other buildings the map leaves out, so each building's share is probably a little high. This is the only real energy number in the whole chain.

2

Olin Hall footprint

Public data + arithmetic
shoelace area of the OpenStreetMap outline x = (lon − lon₀) × 111,320 × cos(lat) y = (lat − lat₀) × 110,574
1,958 m²

The outline is a ring of longitude and latitude points. Degrees are not metres, so each point is converted first: one degree of latitude is about 110,574 m, and one degree of longitude is about 111,320 m times cos(latitude), because longitude lines draw closer together away from the equator. The shoelace formula then turns the corner points into a ground area.

3

Floors and floor area

Public data + arithmetic
floors = max(OpenStreetMap floors, highest floor implied by room numbers, 1) area = footprint × floors
3 floors · 5,873 m²

Olin's rooms (for example 341) imply floor 3, and OpenStreetMap also says 3. Floor area stands in for the building's total size, because no floor plans are public.

4

Class hours per week

Public data
sum of every class meeting's length in the building, each weekday separately
248.3 h

Courses meeting in the same room at the same time count once, so this is real occupied room time. I use it as a stand-in for how heavily the building is used.

5

Class-hour density

Arithmetic
density = 248.3 h ÷ 5,873 m² = 0.0423 per m² average of all scheduled buildings = 0.0288 per m²
0.0423 vs 0.0288

Olin is used about 1.47 times as intensely as the average scheduled building (0.0423 ÷ 0.0288).

6

Activity factor

Assumption (70/30 split)
factor = (1 − s) + s × density ÷ average, with s = 0.30 = 0.70 + 0.30 × (0.0423 ÷ 0.0288)
1.141

I assume 70% of a building's electricity runs regardless of classes (lights, ventilation, servers) and 30% rises and falls with class use. There is no public data to set that split; it is my guess, and it can be changed. A building with average use gets exactly 1.0, and buildings with no classes get 1.0.

7

Olin Hall's electricity per year

Arithmetic
weight = area × factor = 5,873 × 1.141 = 6,702 (all buildings together: 100,781) share = 6,702 ÷ 100,781 = 6.65% kWh = 14,000,000 × 6.65%
931,068 kWh · 158.5 kWh per m²

The campus average is about 139 kWh per m²; Olin is higher because it is busier. By construction, all buildings together add up to exactly 14,000,000 kWh.

Part 2. From the building to one classroom

Steps 8 to 12
8

Room 215's use factor

Assumption (50/50)
scheduled 19.8 h a week; average Olin classroom 16.6 h a week use factor = (1 − u) + u × 19.8 ÷ 16.6, with u = 0.50 = 0.50 + 0.50 × 1.193
1.096

Half of a room's estimate is an equal share and half is weighted by how busy the room is. That 50/50 is another assumption.

9

Room 215's electricity per year

Assumption (classroom area)
building kWh per m² × classroom area × intensity multiplier × use factor = 158.5 × 60 × 1.00 × 1.096
10,429 kWh

60 m² is my assumption for a typical classroom, because room sizes are not public; it can be set per building. The multiplier is 1.00 unless you change it for labs or other heavy rooms. As a safety rule a building's rooms can never add up to more than the building; that rule is not triggered here.

10

Average power

Arithmetic
10,429 kWh ÷ 8,760 hours in a year
1.19 kW

The room's electricity spread evenly over every hour of the year.

11

Share of the week the room is in use

Arithmetic
19.8 h ÷ 168 h in a week
0.1176

About 12% of the week.

12

Power with no class and while a class runs

Assumption (70/30)
no class: 1.19 × 0.70 = 0.83 kW class running: 1.19 × (0.70 + 0.30 ÷ 0.1176) = 1.19 × 3.25 = 3.87 kW
0.83 kW / 3.87 kW

The class-driven 30% all happens during the 11.76% of the week the room is in use, so it is concentrated there. Nothing is created or lost: 0.1176 × 3.87 + 0.8824 × 0.83 ≈ 0.455 + 0.732 = 1.19 kW, the average again.

Part 3. From power to Fall 2026 dollars

Steps 13 to 18
13

Class days in Fall 2026

Public data
Aug 24 to Dec 4, minus Sep 7, Oct 12–13, Nov 25–28, counted per weekday
Mon ×13 · Tue ×14 · Wed ×14 · Thu ×14 · Fri ×14

Dates come from the Registrar's academic calendar. Final exams are not counted.

14

Class hours in the term

Arithmetic
for each meeting: its share of the room's real time × class days of that weekday
272.2 h over 7 sections

If several courses meet in a room at the same time (cross-listed or co-taught), the room is in use once and the time is split equally among them. Seven courses in a 2.75-hour block get 2.75 ÷ 7 = 0.39 hours each, so the room's energy is not charged seven times.

15

Electricity while classes run

Arithmetic
272.2 h × 3.87 kW
1,054 kWh

The product is 1,053 kWh before rounding of the displayed inputs.

16

Cost

Public data price
1,054 kWh × $0.1401 per kWh
$147.63

14.01 cents is the Michigan commercial average in the EIA's 2024 table. Albion's actual rate is not public.

17

Credit hours taught in the room

Public data
each section's credit hours = Albion units × 4, shared over its rooms by class hours, summed for room 215
22.0 credit hours

The Registrar's rule is that one unit equals four semester hours. Each lab section (a zero-credit course such as BIOL 195L) is given 0.25 unit, 1 credit hour, and its lecture sections give up the same 0.25 unit.

18

Energy cost per credit hour

Arithmetic
$147.63 ÷ 22.0
$6.71

This is for the credit hours taught in this room. For a section, course or department, add the energy dollars of all its meetings and divide by its credit hours.

What about winter?

Electricity and heating fuel in the cold months
  • Electricity is spread evenly over the days of the year, so December to February carries 90 of 365 days, which is 24.7% of the year's electricity. The model has no winter lighting or electric-heating pattern, so real winter electricity could differ.
  • Heating fuel follows NOAA's heating degree days for Jackson Reynolds Field: December to February has 3,422.9 of the year's 6,469.6. With my assumption that 80% of heating fuel is weather-driven and 20% flat (hot water, kitchens), those three months use 47.3% of the year's heating fuel, and November to March use 71.0%.
  • Heating cost per credit hour cannot be given in dollars: Albion's heating total and fuel price are not public, and which heating system each building uses is not public either. The app shows each building's share of the heating instead, and shows dollars once you type a heating total and a price.
  • A winter or spring term needs that term's schedule. The Registrar lists Fall 2026 as the newest term and has not published Spring 2027, so the classroom cost here covers Fall 2026 only. The same steps apply once it is posted.
